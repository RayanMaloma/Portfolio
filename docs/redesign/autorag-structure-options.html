Four structures, one rule: the product leads

All four kill the long-scroll report. The live prototype is a first-viewport citizen in every option; narrative depth moves into compact interactive regions. Everything uses the approved AutoRAG world — cobalt on light cobalt wash over the global sand, warm ink, neutral surfaces. Wireframe type is a stand-in for Clash Display.

Option A

Persistent split screen

Product left, story right. The live preview owns ~55% of the screen and stays put; the right column carries title, meta chips, the three-line gist, then compact accordions (Problem · My role · Process · Key decisions · Impact). Reading never takes you away from the product.

Option B

Product dashboard

The page borrows the product's own idiom: slim title band, big live preview, and a tabbed side rail (Overview · Problem · Role · Decisions · Impact). One tab visible at a time; the page never grows. Feels like being handed the app with a guided tour attached.

Option C

Layered editorial

The preview is the hero object; the display-scale title and three compact cards (meta · problem · decisions) overlap its edges — the same layering language as your homepage hero. A "focus" toggle slides the cards away for unobstructed product use. One compact band below holds the feature list + next case.

Option D

Slide-based story

Four controlled panels instead of a page: ① title + gist + preview poster, ② problem & role, ③ the 8-questions idea & key decisions, ④ full-stage live product. Advance by buttons, arrow keys, or swipe — never by hijacked scrolling. A numbered rail (01–04) shows position.

Recommendation: Option A, borrowing C's title treatment. A is the only structure where someone can read your reasoning while operating the live product — which is AutoRAG's whole pitch. It hits the 1–2 viewport target, degrades gracefully on mobile, and its accordions absorb the interview content later without redesign. To keep it from feeling like documentation, lift one move from C: let the display-scale "AutoRAG" title overlap the preview frame's edge, in the cobalt world. B is the runner-up if you want maximum product-tool feeling; C if expressiveness outranks unobstructed product use; D only if you want a deliberate deck-style statement piece.
